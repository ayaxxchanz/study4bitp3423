The central exam argument

Start with the problem, not the technology. A technology is worth adopting only when its capabilities address a real need, its benefits can be measured, and the organisation can manage its technical, financial, organisational and user-related feasibility compared with reasonable alternatives.

1 · Identify the problem

Describe the current pain and measurable outcome.

2 · Assess the fit

Test technology capabilities, feasibility, risks and alternatives.

3 · Recommend with evidence

Adopt, pilot, defer or reject for a justified reason.

01Slides 1–5, 18

Problem before technology

  • Identify what is costly, slow, unsafe or unreliable
  • Choose a capability that addresses the problem
  • Define a baseline and measure improvement
  • A novel technology can still be a poor choice

↳ Problem → capability → evidence.

02Slides 6, 20, 23

Blockchain

  • Shared, tamper-evident records across parties
  • Example: supply-chain product provenance
  • Needs shared governance, trustworthy inputs and participation
  • One trusted organisation? A normal database may be simpler

↳ Not every database needs blockchain.

03Slides 8–9, 21

Internet of Things

  • Sensors observe equipment, energy, temperature and usage
  • Enables alerts, remote checks, predictive maintenance
  • Needs placement, accurate readings, connectivity & action owner
  • Threats: insecure devices, outages, maintenance burden

↳ A sensor without response creates little value.

04Slides 10–11, 22

Extended Reality (XR)

  • VR: immersive virtual practice
  • AR: information over physical context
  • MR: digital and real elements interact
  • Evaluate cost, comfort, access and learning improvement

↳ Impressive visuals ≠ better training.

05Slides 12–16

Four feasibility lenses

  • Technical: fit, integration, data, security, support
  • Financial: initial + recurring + change costs
  • Organisational: skills, roles, leadership, workflows
  • User-related: access, trust, privacy, usability

↳ T-F-O-U: technology, finance, organisation, users.

06Slides 13–16

Costs, risk & ethics

  • Initial: devices, software, setup, data preparation
  • Ongoing: support, licences, replacements and upgrades
  • Hidden: training, redesign, temporary productivity loss
  • Check privacy, safety, fairness, accessibility and accountability

↳ Total cost > purchase price.

07Slides 17–18

Seven-step check

  • 1 Problem/baseline • 2 Measurable benefit
  • 3 Technical/security/data fit • 4 Total cost
  • 5 Staff & user readiness • 6 Alternative/risk check
  • 7 Recommend adopt, pilot, defer or reject

↳ A pilot tests assumptions before scaling.

08Slides 19–25

Three cases + evidence

  • Blockchain: traceability among multiple organisations
  • IoT: building use and fault alerts
  • XR: safe equipment training
  • Case method: problem → fit → feasibility → evidence & limitations

↳ Measure outcomes; compare simpler alternatives.

Structured Exam Practice

Past-paper style: a short scenario, numbered subquestions and specific mark allocations. Answer point-by-point, not as a long essay.

PART A15 structured questionsThree practice sets of five questions. Each question: 10 marks, split into (a) and (b).
PART B2 extended case studiesEach case: 25 marks with specific (a), (b) and (c) tasks.
How to answer a structured question

Check the command word (state, identify, explain, discuss, justify), the number of items requested and the allocated marks. For 4 marks, aim for two distinct explained points; for 6 marks, explain three. For a case study, use the facts in the scenario.
