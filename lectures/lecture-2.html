The exam-ready argument

Software can work perfectly in a technical sense and still harm people. Good software engineering asks not only “Does it work?” but also “Who is affected, what can go wrong, and what is responsible?”

1 · Build it right

Technical quality, safety, security and reliability.

2 · Build the right thing

Purpose, users, stakeholders and consequences.

3 · Think beyond the system

Society, sustainability and wider context.

Three engineer questions

  • Can we build it? Feasibility
  • Should we build it? Purpose and ethics
  • What could happen? Impact and unintended effects
  • 92% accurate AI can still label or stigmatise students

↳ Works ≠ wise to deploy.

02Slides 7–10

Ethics ≠ law ≠ policy

  • Ethics: fair, responsible judgement
  • Legal: what law permits or requires
  • Professional: standards & codes
  • Clicking “I Agree” does not remove engineer responsibility

↳ Consent alone is insufficient.

03Slides 10–15

ACM/IEEE areas 1–4

  • 1 Public: prevent harm; consider exclusion
  • 2 Client & employer: honest, lawful service consistent with public interest
  • 3 Product: fit, reliable, secure, safe
  • 4 Judgment: objective decisions, even under deadline pressure

↳ Public interest is fundamental.

04Slides 16–19

ACM/IEEE areas 5–8

  • 5 Management: fair, realistic and ethical planning
  • 6 Profession: preserve integrity, avoid false claims
  • 7 Colleagues: give credit and constructive feedback
  • 8 Self: lifelong learning and competence

↳ People and practice matter.

05Slides 20–22

Trade-offs & 7 checks

  • Trade-offs: speed/safety • cost/quality • privacy/convenience
  • Plus personalisation/data collection • automation/human control
  • Check: affected? wrong? benefits? who risks?
  • Also: informed users? safer option? defensible decision?

↳ Do not choose speed blindly.

06Slides 23–31

Ripple & stakeholders

  • Impact spreads: user → organisation → others → community → society
  • Invisible: non-users, families, vulnerable groups, environment
  • Digital-only services may exclude people without access
  • Map benefit, harm, accountability & mitigation

↳ Non-users count too.

SDGs + evidence

  • SDG 3 health • 4 education • 9 innovation
  • 10 inequality • 11 cities • 12 consumption • 13 climate
  • Smart waste: sensors → route efficiency → reduced waste/fuel
  • Explain the mechanism; never just name an SDG

↳ Link action to measurable outcome.

Malaysia + conclusion

  • MySTIE: innovation + socioeconomic needs

  • Industry4WRD: fit within industry priorities
  • Build it right • Build the right thing • Think beyond

↳ Use four lenses: ethical, social, global, national.

Structured Exam Practice

Past-paper style: a short scenario, numbered subquestions and specific mark allocations. Answer point-by-point, not as a long essay.

PART A15 structured questionsThree practice sets of five questions. Each question: 10 marks, split into (a) and (b).
PART B2 extended case studiesEach case: 25 marks with specific (a), (b) and (c) tasks.
How to answer a structured question

Check the command word (state, identify, explain, discuss, justify), the number of items requested and the allocated marks. For 4 marks, aim for two distinct explained points; for 6 marks, explain three. For a case study, use the facts in the scenario. The suggested full answers, marking points and mark allocations are practice aids, not official answer schemes.
